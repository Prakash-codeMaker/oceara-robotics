# Oceara O-01 — Hydropower Customer Discovery Pack

**Revision:** 0.1 — 20 November 2026  
**Goal:** Validate the operational problem before claiming product-market fit.

## 1. Interview target

Speak with maintenance, hydro-mechanical, civil, dam-safety, instrumentation, inspection and procurement personnel at hydropower stations. Include both asset owners/operators and underwater inspection contractors.

The initial 15-interview target is a research goal, not a claim that interviews have occurred.

## 2. Opening statement

“Oceara is developing a pre-prototype tethered underwater inspection ROV. We are researching how stations inspect submerged intake structures and trash racks, and what evidence engineers need. We do not yet have a built or field-tested vehicle. This conversation is for understanding your current process and constraints, not selling a ready product.”

## 3. Interview questions

### Current inspection process
1. Which submerged structures are inspected at this station?
2. How is each inspection currently performed (diver, dewatering, camera, sonar, contractor or another method)?
3. How frequently are trash racks and intake structures inspected?
4. What usually triggers an additional inspection?
5. Who plans, performs and reviews the inspection?

### Operational pain
6. Which parts are most difficult: current, turbulence, visibility, debris, access, depth, tether/umbilical, safety, downtime or another factor?
7. What conditions prevent an inspection from producing useful evidence?
8. What is the consequence when a panel or component cannot be inspected adequately?
9. How long does mobilization and inspection typically take?
10. What causes outage, generation loss or maintenance delay, if any?

### Evidence and decisions
11. What does the engineer need to see in a report?
12. Are rack panels referenced by drawing, bay, grid or another numbering system?
13. Are photos/video, depth/location, timestamps or condition ratings required?
14. Which standards, site procedures or approval workflows govern inspection?
15. Who is qualified to classify a suspected defect and approve the report?

### Procurement and pilot
16. Who owns the budget and who approves a trial?
17. Is inspection bought as a service or performed using owned equipment?
18. What would a controlled mock-up demonstration need to prove?
19. What would make a supervised site pilot acceptable or unacceptable?
20. What commercial model is realistic: paid inspection service, rental, equipment purchase, annual support or contractor partnership?

## 4. Evidence to capture

For every interview, record:
- date, role and organization (with permission);
- present method and frequency;
- exact problem example;
- operational impact and evidence source;
- must-have report fields;
- site constraints and safety approvals;
- buyer, budget owner and procurement route;
- pilot acceptance criteria;
- follow-up owner and agreed next step.

Do not record confidential plant details without permission. Do not turn a polite conversation into a claimed partnership or endorsement.

## 5. Problem-validation scorecard

Score each dimension 0–2 with a written rationale:
- problem occurs repeatedly;
- present method has a material limitation;
- evidence is insufficient or difficult to compare;
- an operator can identify a budget owner;
- a mock-rack test would be useful;
- a route to a supervised pilot exists.

A high score is a reason to investigate further, not proof of purchase intent.

## 6. Proposed discovery milestones

- 5 interviews: confirm vocabulary, process and inspection workflow.
- 10 interviews: identify recurring operational limitations and customer segments.
- 15 interviews: compare needs by asset type and rank purchase criteria.
- 3 potential design partners: request written problem statements or non-binding letters of interest only where genuine interest exists.

## 7. Interview follow-up

Send a short thank-you, summarize what was learned, ask permission before quoting the person, and request an introduction only when appropriate.
