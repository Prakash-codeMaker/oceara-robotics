## 1. Executive summary

Oceara Robotics proposes to develop O-01, a compact tethered underwater remotely operated vehicle for visual inspection of submerged hydropower intake structures and trash racks. The first prototype will focus on operator-supervised movement, stable viewing, HD video and adjustable lighting, depth/orientation sensing, tethered communication and panel-wise inspection evidence.

The project is at the pre-prototype stage. An initial system architecture, product requirements, preliminary component estimates and concept website exist. No physical vehicle, controlled-water test results, industrial validation or paying customer pilots are claimed.

The proposed project will produce a working physical demonstrator, conduct staged bench and controlled-water tests, and demonstrate a repeatable mock trash-rack inspection workflow. All observations will remain subject to qualified engineer review.

## 2. Problem statement

Submerged intake structures and trash racks can be difficult to inspect consistently because visibility, flow, access, debris, positioning and tether management affect the quality of inspection. Inspection evidence may be distributed across video, photographs, dive notes and maintenance records.

O-01 will investigate whether a portable tethered inspection platform can improve the repeatability and traceability of visual evidence in controlled and later supervised environments. The operational problem, buyer requirements and willingness to pay remain under customer discovery and must not be represented as validated market facts.

## 3. Proposed innovation

The proposed system combines:
- compact tethered underwater propulsion platform;
- operator-controlled movement with staged assistance;
- camera and adjustable lighting;
- depth and orientation measurements;
- surface-side control and safe shutdown;
- synchronized inspection video and metadata;
- software to organize evidence by rack panel and generate an engineer-reviewable report.

The initial differentiating hypothesis is an inspection-focused, portable and cost-conscious system with attention to repeatable positioning and evidence management. This is a hypothesis to test against existing suppliers and inspection contractors, not a proven competitive advantage.

## 4. Scope and exclusions

**Included:** engineering design, frame and enclosure fabrication, propulsion, tether/power, camera/lighting, depth/orientation sensing, basic control integration, inspection logging, mock-rack rig and controlled tests.

**Excluded from the initial prototype:** autonomous operation in live infrastructure, AI-based defect diagnosis, manipulator/gripper/debris extraction, long-range tunnel inspection and imaging sonar unless separately justified and approved.

## 8. Risks and mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Insufficient station-keeping | Prioritize thrust/control testing, buoyancy/trim and tether-force assessment |
| Tether drag or entanglement | Design tether routing, strain relief and controlled payout early |
| Poor optical visibility | Lighting trials; evaluate imaging sonar as a separate payload if justified |
| Enclosure leakage | Independent sealing and pressure qualification before integrated wet tests |
| Sensor drift or misalignment | Calibration, metadata quality flags and conservative positioning claims |
| Supplier cost/lead-time changes | Alternative vendor evaluation and staged procurement |
| Access to test facilities | Secure permission and controlled test partner before wet-testing |
| Student/NOC/IP constraints | Obtain written institutional NOC and project-completion commitment; clarify IP ownership before submission |
